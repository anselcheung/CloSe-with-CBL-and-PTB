\section{CLIP-Initialized Codebook}

\subsection{Setup}

Instead of a random initialization, the garment codebook's 18 rows are seeded from the
frozen OpenAI CLIP text encoder's embeddings of each class name (prompt: ``a photo of''
plus the lowercased class name), projected down to the codebook dimension via a fixed
random projection, and left trainable during training. This tests whether a semantic
prior over garment classes is a cheaper or complementary route to the same
boundary-quality gains as CBL/PTB/BRM/SegFix.

\subsection{Discussion}

Within the CLIP-initialized campaign, most individual contributions leave aggregate B-IoU comparable to or worse than the CLIP baseline (Table~8.2); the exception is CBL at +1.15 pp vs.\ the CLIP baseline. On the tail classes, CLIP-alone clearly lags PTB+BRM+CBL (Dress 10.2 $\pm$ 1.38 pp, Jumpsuits 12.0 $\pm$ 2.34 pp, Scarf 13.0 $\pm$ 0.26 pp; Table~8.3), but combining both matches PTB+BRM+CBL on the tail (no detectable per-class difference there) while adding no detectable aggregate B-IoU (+0.40 $\pm$ 0.28 pp vs.\ PTB+BRM+CBL alone). This is consistent with CLIP shaping the codebook in a way that helps common-class boundaries but does not resolve the hard cases the boundary-supervision methods target. Under CLIP init, PTB's effect remains negative and measurable (-3.23 $\pm$ 0.42 pp), and BRM's becomes detectable (-0.36 $\pm$ 0.14 pp). Neither is eliminated by CLIP -- both continue to trade aggregate B-IoU for tail-class gains, just as they did under random init. CBL vs.\ its own baseline is the only individual contribution with a positive point estimate under CLIP init (+1.15 $\pm$ 0.65 pp, though -- like every individual contribution in Table~8.2 -- it falls just short of this report's $2\sigma$ detectability bar); under random init the same comparison is smaller and clearly undetectable (-0.53 $\pm$ 0.37 pp).
